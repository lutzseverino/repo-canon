# Exceptional slices

Read the applicable section when drafting wide refactors, deepening tickets or
a cross-repository switch. Ordinary tracer bullets stay in [the method](SKILL.md).

## Wide refactors

**Wide refactors are an exception to vertical slicing.** A **wide refactor** is one mechanical change (rename a column, retype a shared symbol) whose **blast radius** fans across the whole codebase, so a single edit breaks thousands of call sites at once and no vertical slice can land green. Don't force it into a tracer bullet; sequence it as **expand–contract**. First expand: add the new form beside the old so nothing breaks. Then migrate the call sites over in batches sized by blast radius (per package, per directory), each batch its own ticket blocked by the expand, keeping CI green batch to batch because the old form still exists. Finally contract: delete the old form once no caller remains, in a ticket blocked by every migrate batch. Every ticket's change merges into its base branch on its own, with its checks passing: when even the batches can't stay green alone, fold the batches that can't into one ticket.

## Deepening tickets

**Deepening tickets are the other exception to vertical slicing.** A **deepening ticket** restructures code into a deeper module, in `codebase-design`'s vocabulary, and makes no new end-to-end behaviour work. Its **What to build** states the structural outcome, then `Behaviour changes: none` or each behaviour change listed, never a promise that behaviour is unchanged. It carries one acceptance criterion naming the type, parameter or function that must no longer exist. That criterion names the thing itself, never a line count or an inline block, which describe today's structure; when the thing is a durable surface, the ticket's `Deletes:` line names it too. Tests observing the deepened module's external interface keep passing without assertion changes, except where a listed behaviour change requires one; tests of the shallow modules it removes are replaced, as `codebase-design`'s replace-don't-layer testing strategy says. Several deepening tickets over one area order by the [overlapping-writes rule](SKILL.md#2-draft-vertical-slices).

## Cross-repository switches

**A cross-repository switch waits on its activation steps.** A ticket **switches** one side of a cross-repository break when its change, once merged, released or adopted, activates a protocol, configuration or workflow change that a deployed producer or consumer in another repository, or a host, cannot yet handle. Each **activation step** the switch needs, whether a release, an adopter update, a host step or the recovery path for a repository the switch deliberately pauses, is a ticket of its own, published in the repository that owns that step. Join the activation steps to the switching ticket with blocking edges: every activation step that must land before the switch blocks it, so the switching ticket stays behind them on the frontier, and an activation step that can only follow the switch, such as recovering a paused repository, is blocked by it. The split's evidence for independent review checks the activation steps against each intervening deployed state: who can still deliver each remaining activation step, the merge tooling on the destination repository's base, contracts and results that are already ready, and adopters' installed versions. A host-step ticket names the step and its outcome; host names, providers and access mechanisms stay in owner setup. A later change to the activation steps is an amendment under [Gather context](SKILL.md#1-gather-context).
